# Entrepreneurial Learning and Critical Reflection: Discontinuous Events as Triggers for 'Higher-level' Learning

**Author:** Jason Cope
**Year:** 2003
**Source type:** article
**Raw file:** [Cope 2003.pdf](../../raw/articles/Cope%202003.pdf)

## Summary
This article builds a deeper understanding of the learning outcomes triggered by significant, discontinuous events during the entrepreneurial process. Based on qualitative case study research into the 'lived experience' of six practising entrepreneurs, it argues that there is more to learning from discontinuous events than the incremental accumulation of more routinized, habitual, 'lower-level' learning. The article illustrates that these events have the capacity to stimulate distinctive forms of 'higher-level' learning — including 'double-loop' learning (Argyris & Schön) and 'transformative' learning (Mezirow) — and suggests that these learning outcomes are the result of 'inward' critical self-reflection.

## Key Claims
- There is more to learning from discontinuous events than the incremental accumulation of more routinized, habitual, 'lower-level' learning (p. 429).
- Discontinuous events have the capacity to stimulate distinctive forms of 'higher-level' learning that are fundamental to the entrepreneur in both personal and business terms (p. 429).
- Lower-level learning is focused learning that may be mere repetition of past behaviours — usually short-term, surface, temporary (Fiol & Lyles, 1985) (p. 433).
- Higher-level learning is the development of complex rules and associations regarding new actions, and the development of an understanding of causation — double-loop learning, where central norms, frames of reference and assumptions are changed (Fiol & Lyles, 1985) (p. 433).
- Single-loop learning is a more adaptive form of learning that may challenge and 'correct' or 'modify' an individual's existing strategies and assumptions, but the underlying 'theories for action' remain unchanged (p. 432).
- Double-loop learning has the capacity to fundamentally change an individual's theories for action, thereby creating entirely new assumptions and strategies for effective action (p. 432).
- Transformative learning involves 'an enhanced level of awareness of the context of one's beliefs and feelings' and 'profound changes in self' (Mezirow, 1991) (p. 437).
- Transformative learning is triggered by a 'disorienting dilemma' or 'crisis' (Mezirow, 1990) (p. 440).
- The catalyst for transformative learning can sometimes be an event that is largely self-imposed and is the result of a clear mistake made by the entrepreneur (p. 441).
- The higher-order learning outcomes described are inextricably linked with, and are the outcome of, 'critical reflection' (p. 443).
- The reflection engaged in was not 'critical' from the Critical Theory perspective, but more precisely 'inward' critical self-reflection (Kemmis, 1985) (p. 444).
- The 'richness' of the learning experience may well be greater if discontinuous learning events trigger both double-loop and transformative learning outcomes (p. 440).
