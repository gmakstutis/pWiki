# Rethinking Professional Development Observations of HE Lecturers: Cases of the Unorthodox

**Author:** Martin Compton
**Year:** 2019
**Source type:** article
**Raw file:** [Compton.pdf](../../raw/articles/Compton.pdf)

## Summary

This doctoral thesis (EdD, UCL Institute of Education) examines three cases of unorthodox approaches to observation of teaching and learning (OTL) used for lecturer development in higher education: a model that widens the vista of observation beyond face-to-face teaching and asserts particular value in observer learning (Peer Supported Review); a system that extends and revitalises 'microteaching' (Extended Microteaching); and a scheme where students review teaching and ancillary work of lecturers (Student Reviewers). Using Activity Theory as a framework, the study finds that breaking from the orthodoxy necessarily reflects the culture of the institution, can lead to positive and sometimes unanticipated outcomes, and reinforces the imperative to question the underpinning purpose and design of all observation schemes. The thesis presents an original analytical framework, the '4 Ms Observation Audit' (Milieu, Motive, Mediator, Mechanism), for appraising and creating OTL schemes.

## Key Claims

- Observation of teaching and learning (OTL) serves a broad array of purposes, embracing evaluative observations by senior colleagues, comparatively low-stakes developmental peer observation, and mixed-purpose observations (p. 21).
- Most OTL schemes share common elements: Person A watches person B, makes notes or fills in a form, and gives person B feedback — conceptualised as an orthodox approach to OTL (p. 21).
- Breaking from the orthodoxy necessarily reflects the culture of the institution, can lead to positive (and sometimes unanticipated) outcomes, and reinforces the imperative to question underpinning purpose and design of all observation schemes (p. 2-3).
- Surveillance, compulsion, voluntarism, collegiality and developing self-efficacy are all key lenses of the analysis (p. 3).
- There needs to be a separation of QA and QE OTL if its developmental potential is to be realised (p. 172).
- The benefits of observer learning take a subordinate role to observee learning in all three cases (p. 174).
- We can be shackled by assumption and convention when it comes to OTL (p. 174).
- The '4 Ms' model (Milieu, Motive, Mediator, Mechanism) can be reconfigured as a simple auditing tool for analysing existing OTL schemes (p. 176).
- By offering a conceptual orthodoxy, the thesis foregrounds and celebrates 'unorthodoxy', offering channels for innovation that unify by difference (p. 175).
- Observation anxiety and resistance transcend evaluative OTL but impediments can be diminished by positive experience, repetition and a clearly-articulated, subject-oriented rationale (p. 4).
