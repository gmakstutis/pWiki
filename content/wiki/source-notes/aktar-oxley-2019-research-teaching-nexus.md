# Promoting Student Engagement by Strengthening the Link Between Research and Teaching in Higher Education

**Author:** Tasnima Aktar & Laura Oxley
**Year:** 2019
**Source type:** article
**Raw file:** [Aktar & Oxley 2019.pdf](../../raw/articles/Aktar%20&%20Oxley%202019.pdf)

## Summary

Two early-career researchers reflect on their experience as Graduate Teaching Assistants (GTAs) at the University of York and their participation in the York Learning and Teaching Award (YLTA), a UK Professional Standards Framework (PSF)-informed programme. They argue that strengthening the link between research and teaching promotes positive student engagement, drawing on Kolb's experiential learning cycle as their primary model of reflection. The article advocates the research-teaching nexus as a way to make lessons more authentic and motivating for students.

## Key Claims

- Linking research to teaching makes lessons more authentic and interesting, promoting student engagement (p. 170).
- Student engagement is "the first and foremost issue of successful teaching" and correlates robustly with positive outcomes including academic achievement and satisfaction (p. 170).
- Healey and Jenkins (2009) distinguish four ways of linking research and teaching: research-led, research-oriented, research-based, and research-tutored — varying by whether students are active participants or audience, and whether focus is on content or process (p. 172).
- The four ways are not independent; a single module may combine elements of more than one approach (p. 176-177).
- Students become "producers of knowledge not just consumers" when engaged in research-based teaching, exposing them to experiential learning (p. 174).
- The REF and TEF are separate frameworks with distinct criteria, but linking research to teaching can drive improvement in both; separate policies risk creating a "false dichotomy" (p. 171).
- A gulf exists between research-focussed and teaching-focussed academics, with two distinct communities developing (Tierney, 2016) (p. 171).
- Collaborative strategies (group problem solving, pair discussion, buzz groups) informed by Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development foster critical thinking and deep learning (p. 175).
- Teachers can motivate students by sharing personal research knowledge and experience, fostering beneficial interaction (p. 175).
