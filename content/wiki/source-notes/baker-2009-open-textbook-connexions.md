# Open Textbook Proof-of-Concept via Connexions

**Author:** Judy Baker, Joel Thierstein, Kathi Fletcher, Manpreet Kaur & Jonathan Emmons
**Year:** 2009
**Source type:** article
**Raw file:** [Baker et al 2009.pdf](../../raw/articles/Baker%20et%20al%202009.pdf)

## Summary

This article documents a proof-of-concept collaboration between Rice University's Connexions and the Community College Open Textbook Project (CCOTP) to develop a free and open textbook. The proof-of-concept served to document a workflow process that would support adoption of open textbooks, addressing the high cost of textbooks as a barrier to college for many students. It identifies challenges to the production and adoption of open textbooks and lessons learned about production and use.

## Key Claims

- Community colleges educate 44% of the nation's undergraduates and 50% of the nation's teachers; for many of those students, affordability is critical (p. 2).
- The high cost of textbooks presents a barrier to college for many students; in the absence of meaningful solutions, some students are resorting to behaviors that undermine academic integrity (p. 2).
- A nationwide survey of 1,203 faculty members found a large gap between those willing to use open educational resources (OER) in their classes (91%) and those actively using OER (34%) (p. 2).
- The dual challenges of locating and inspiring the use of fully vetted, high-quality OER and open textbooks contribute to this gap (p. 2).
- Challenges to the production and adoption of open textbooks include: 1) faculty members' and students' expectations of high production quality and ancillaries, 2) methods for documenting and maintaining control over various versions, and 3) the process of converting existing open content to digital and accessible formats (p. 2).
- Often publishers' textbooks are inappropriate for community college courses because they contain far more information than is needed, or generic information that lacks regional, local, or cultural relevance (p. 3).
- Fully open educational resources provide a license granting permission not only to read but also to download, modify, and post for reuse; the capacity and right to reuse materials is an important step in providing users the opportunity to actively participate in the open education resources teaching and learning processes (p. 3).
- Reuse makes possible continuous cycles of improvement of educational materials, creating an environment for the improvement of content similar to open source software (p. 3).
- Connexions distinguishes itself from other learning content repositories by providing authors with a way to copy, customize, share, and disseminate open textbooks rather than merely providing access (p. 4).
- Lessons learned about open textbook production: the importance of a style guide, the advantage of assembly-line workflow, the importance of naming conventions and standard math authoring tools, and the importance of customizing print formatting (p. 8).
- Lessons learned about open textbook use: the value of availability and customizability, the importance of interactivity, the difference in how faculty and students view modularity, and the importance of textbook reading navigational aids (p. 8).
- Instructors and students viewed modularity very differently: instructors believed the book was too modularized and broke up the flow of their lessons, while students claimed modularity as a benefit (p. 10).
