# An Investigation into the Comparative Learning Gain and 'Value Added' for Students from Widening Participation and Non-Widening Participation Groups: A Case Study from Sports Degrees

**Author:** Andrea Cameron, Yvette Wharton & Jennifer B. Scally
**Year:** 2018
**Source type:** article
**Raw file:** [Cameron et al 2018.pdf](../../raw/articles/Cameron%20et%20al%202018.pdf)

## Summary
This case study examines the graduate outcomes of widening participation (WP) and non-WP students graduating from Abertay University's sport programmes in the years 2000–2015, using an e-mail survey and departmental database of graduate destinations linked with the student record. Analysis confirmed that those from WP backgrounds were equally as likely to gain a good degree as their non-WP counterparts and to be in graduate and/or sports employment, providing evidence of the 'value added' aspect of learning gain. The paper considers longitudinal graduate outcomes in the context of pedagogic strategy, particularly mandatory placement and employability interventions.

## Key Claims
- Learning gain can be considered from the dual perspectives of 'distance travelled' and 'value-added' (Houreau McGrath et al., 2015); the 'value added' aspect is based on an 'exceeding expectation' ethos (p. 84).
- Graduate employment is considered by some to be a proxy measure for learning gain (p. 84).
- WP graduates compared to their non-WP peers earn less and are less likely to be promoted (Social Mobility Commission, 2017) (p. 83).
- Abertay sport graduates from SIMD20/40, 'first in family' homes, and from FE were equally likely to exit with a good degree as their counterparts (p. 95).
- There were no differences in graduate and/or sports employment rates for those from SIMD20/40, 'first in family' homes, and from FE; and they were equally likely to have felt prepared for their first job and for working life in general (p. 95).
- Placement and research methods were the most cited aspects of the graduates' studies that had helped them make the transition to employment (p. 95).
- During the study period those who accessed the Abertay sport programmes from FE were significantly less likely to progress to postgraduate study (p. 97).
- The key influence on a graduate's future, regardless of the university attended, is socioeconomic background (Universities UK, 2016) (p. 86).
- 'Reconceiving habitus' (Clarke, 2017) may underlie some of the 'time spent at Abertay' graduate responses with regard to enablers for working life (p. 98).
- The curriculum needs to keep providing relevant opportunities for students to develop skill sets and networks that enable graduate employment, particularly as WP students represent a greater proportion of the population (p. 99).
