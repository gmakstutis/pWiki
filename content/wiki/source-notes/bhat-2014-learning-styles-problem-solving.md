# The Effect of Learning Styles on Problem Solving Ability among High School Students

**Author:** Mehraj Ahmad Bhat
**Year:** 2014
**Source type:** article
**Raw file:** [Bhat 2014.pdf](../../raw/articles/Bhat%202014.pdf)

## Summary

This research characterized the preferred learning styles of a sample of 598 high school students (grade 10) in South Kashmir, Jammu and Kashmir, and investigated which preferred learning style had better problem solving ability. Using Kolb's Learning Style Inventory and L.N. Dubey's Problem Solving Ability Test, the study found the most preferred learning style was Assimilator, followed by Accommodator, Diverger, and Converger, and that assimilator learning style had better problem solving ability than the others.

## Key Claims

- Learning styles are relatively stable preferences used by each individual to organise and process information for problem solving or to approach a learning task (p. 1).
- The term 'learning style' and 'cognitive style' are often used interchangeably, though researchers consider learning style the broader construct (p. 1).
- Learning styles are not inborn characteristics but are developed through experience (p. 2).
- Kolb structures the learning process in a four-stage model encompassing two continuums: the concrete-abstract continuum (how people perceive new information) and the active-reflective continuum (how we process new information) (p. 2).
- The four learning styles are: Converging (abstract conceptualization and active experimentation), Diverging (concrete experience and reflective observation), Assimilating (abstract conceptualization and reflective observation), and Accommodating (concrete experience and active experimentation) (p. 2-3).
- The most preferred learning style among high school students was Assimilator (28.3%), followed by Accommodator (28.1%), Diverger (25.4%), and Converger (18.2%) (p. 3).
- Learning style influenced the problem solving ability of high school students (F = 12.29, significant at 0.01 level) (p. 4).
- The assimilator learning style showed the best problem solving ability (mean 12.00), followed by diverger (11.64), accommodator (10.48), and converger (10.19) (p. 4).
- Students having assimilating learning style prefer abstract conceptualization and reflective observation, enabling them to understand a wider range of information and organize it into a concise and logical form (p. 4-5).
- Two major strategies have been proposed for enhancing students' achievement: providing learning environments that match students' learning styles, and teaching for a balanced use of styles or flexibility (p. 5).
- Flexibility is crucial for students as well as for teachers (p. 5).
