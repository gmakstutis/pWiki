# Formative vs Summative Assessment

## Framing
This debate concerns the purpose of assessment: whether it should be formative (to improve teaching and learning through feedback) or summative (to judge performance for accountability and decision-making). At stake is how assessment results are used, who they serve, and whether the two functions can coexist without undermining each other.

## Positions

### Position A: Assessment Should Be Formative
Formative assessment is a "feedback loop in which teaching influences learning, learning influences outcomes, and assessment of outcomes is used to influence/improve teaching and, ultimately, learning." It involves giving regular feedback to students and facilitating program modifications and increased teaching effectiveness.
Key texts: [Nusche 2008](../source-notes/nusche-2008-assessment-learning-outcomes.md)

### Position B: Assessment Should Be Summative
Summative assessment aims at responding to accountability demands of some organizationally higher authority, involving judging performance for a decision or record. It can have a sorting function at the individual level and serve national or state accountability frameworks at the institutional level.
Key texts: [Nusche 2008](../source-notes/nusche-2008-assessment-learning-outcomes.md)

## Key Texts
- [Nusche 2008](../source-notes/nusche-2008-assessment-learning-outcomes.md) — presents both positions
- [Deeley et al 2019](../source-notes/deeley-2019-wicked-problem-assessment-feedback.md) — complicates the dichotomy, noting summative assessment can also provide learning opportunities
- [Poet et al 2018](../source-notes/poet-2018-assessment-without-levels.md) — evidence from English schools on the shift toward formative assessment after removing levels

## Current State
The debate is ongoing. Only Brazil and some federal US states make accreditation and resource allocation directly contingent on satisfactory results in standardized outcomes assessments, while most countries use assessment primarily for formative purposes.
