# Developmental vs Evaluative Observation

## Framing
This debate concerns whether observation of teaching should be developmental (formative, for the observed teacher's own improvement) or evaluative (summative, for assessment and accountability). At stake is the purpose of observation, the nature of feedback, and whether observation can serve both functions without compromising either.

## Positions

### Position A: Observation is Developmental
Peer observation is regarded by academics and educational developers as a tool for personal and professional development. The observer is not there to assess teaching; they provide feedback to enable the observed to assess their own teaching. Outcomes are confidential between participants unless both agree to make them public.
Key texts: [An Introduction to Peer Observation of Teaching](../source-notes/introduction-peer-observation-teaching.md)

### Position B: Observation is Evaluative
There is pressure for observation to serve accountability and quality assurance purposes, where the observer makes summative judgements about teaching quality. This is the tension identified between evaluation and peer review models.
Key texts: [An Introduction to Peer Observation of Teaching](../source-notes/introduction-peer-observation-teaching.md)

## Key Texts
- [An Introduction to Peer Observation of Teaching](../source-notes/introduction-peer-observation-teaching.md) — supports Position A, acknowledges the tension with Position B
- [Compton 2019](../source-notes/compton-2019-unorthodox-observation.md) — argues for a separation of QA and QE OTL, supports Position A while examining unorthodox developmental approaches
- [Danko et al 2016](../source-notes/danko-2016-peer-observation-change.md) — supports Position A, describing observation rather than evaluating

## Current State
The debate is ongoing. The guide advocates for the developmental model while acknowledging the institutional pressure toward evaluation.
