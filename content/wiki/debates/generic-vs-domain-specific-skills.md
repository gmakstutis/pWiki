# Generic vs Domain-Specific Skills

## Framing
This debate concerns whether skills such as critical thinking, communication, and problem-solving are generic (transferable across disciplines) or domain-specific (tied to particular fields of study). At stake is how these skills should be taught, assessed, and valued, and whether higher education can be held accountable for developing them.

## Positions

### Position A: Skills are Generic and Transferable
Generic skills outcomes transcend disciplines and are transferable between different subject areas and contextual situations. Employers value cross-cutting skills as more important to success than the undergraduate major. Assessments focusing on generic skills allow comparison across courses and institutions.
Key texts: [Andrade 2020](../source-notes/andrade-2020-cross-cutting-skills.md) · [Nusche 2008](../source-notes/nusche-2008-assessment-learning-outcomes.md)

### Position B: Skills are Domain-Specific
There is disagreement as to whether thinking processes are generic or field-specific. Domain-specific skills are the thinking patterns used within a broad disciplinary domain, and are not entirely transferable. Some argue that although academic skills are a general outcome of higher education, they can hardly be tested independently of disciplinary subject matters.
Key texts: [Nusche 2008](../source-notes/nusche-2008-assessment-learning-outcomes.md)

## Key Texts
- [Andrade 2020](../source-notes/andrade-2020-cross-cutting-skills.md) — supports Position A
- [Nusche 2008](../source-notes/nusche-2008-assessment-learning-outcomes.md) — presents both positions

## Current State
The debate is ongoing. There is a risk that generic skills assessment may end up measuring students' intelligence and results of prior schooling more than the impact of higher education, complicating the assessment of generic skills.
