# Criterion vs Norm Referencing

## Framing

This debate concerns whether student work should be graded against explicit criteria and standards (criterion referencing) or in comparison to other students in the cohort (norm referencing). At stake is the reliability and fairness of marking, the transparency of assessment, and whether published criteria actually guide grading decisions.

## Positions

### Position A: Criterion Referencing is Best Practice

The accountability paradigm advocates that assessment should be based on criterion rather than norm referencing, so that a student is judged against a set of standards, not against his or her peers. The production, publication, and discussion of clear assessment criteria is regarded as a sine qua non of an effective assessment strategy.
Key texts: [Bloxham et al 2011](../source-notes/bloxham-2011-assessment-criteria-grading.md)

### Position B: Criterion Referencing is Unrealistic; Norm Referencing is Inevitable

The distinction between criterion and norm referencing is unhelpful. Assessors' grading behaviour is tacitly influenced by norm referencing, and tutors draw on their knowledge of different students' work to make their judgements. Two-thirds of markers in the study used some form of norm referencing. Written criteria only take on meaning once staff apply their personal 'standards framework', and other students' work is crucial in forming that framework.
Key texts: [Bloxham et al 2011](../source-notes/bloxham-2011-assessment-criteria-grading.md)

## Key Texts

- [Bloxham et al 2011](../source-notes/bloxham-2011-assessment-criteria-grading.md) — supports Position B, presents Position A as the policy paradigm
- [Bloxham 2009](../source-notes/bloxham-2009-marking-moderation.md) — supports Position B

## Current State

The debate is ongoing. The authors argue that marking standards are social constructions, and that quality assurance should focus on building shared understanding of marking standards rather than demonstrating written guidance.
