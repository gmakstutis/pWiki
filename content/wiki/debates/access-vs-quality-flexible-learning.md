# Access vs Quality in Flexible Learning

## Framing

This debate concerns whether the expansion of flexible learning (online, blended, competency-based, OER) to increase access to higher education can be achieved without compromising quality. At stake is whether flexible delivery modes can produce learning outcomes comparable to or better than traditional face-to-face instruction, and how institutions can scale flexible learning sustainably.

## Positions

### Position A: Flexible Learning Risks Quality

Concerns about quality and student success in online courses persist. Nearly 45% of chief academic officers view retention in online courses as more difficult than in face-to-face courses, and 68.3% believe the former requires greater discipline. Some institutions respond by disallowing certain populations from taking online courses.
Key texts: [Andrade & Alden-Rivers 2019](../source-notes/andrade-alden-rivers-2019-flexible-learning-framework.md)

### Position B: Flexible Learning Can Achieve Quality Through Design

Success for all students in flexible forms of delivery can be achieved through course design and responsive pedagogies, supported by competency frameworks and training for teaching staff. Quality assurance processes (peer review, checkpoints) can make online courses more rigorous than face-to-face courses, which do not typically undergo similar review.
Key texts: [Andrade & Alden-Rivers 2019](../source-notes/andrade-alden-rivers-2019-flexible-learning-framework.md)

## Key Texts

- [Andrade & Alden-Rivers 2019](../source-notes/andrade-alden-rivers-2019-flexible-learning-framework.md) — supports Position B, presents Position A as a concern
- [Baker et al 2009](../source-notes/baker-2009-open-textbook-connexions.md) — documents OER/open-textbook adoption, supporting Position B on access

## Current State

The debate is ongoing. The authors argue that blocking students from online coursework will not improve outcomes, and that quality can be assured through intentional design and quality assurance processes.

This debate matters for Paulo's interest in how access to education and skill development can be expanded without compromising the quality of learning, relevant to questions of who gets to learn and how.
