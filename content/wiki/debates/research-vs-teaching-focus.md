# Research vs Teaching Focus

## Framing
This debate concerns whether research and teaching in higher education are distinct, competing activities or interconnected and mutually reinforcing. At stake is how institutions allocate resources, how academics are evaluated (REF vs TEF), and whether the "research-teaching nexus" is a real phenomenon or a false dichotomy.

## Positions

### Position A: Research and Teaching are Distinct
Some argue that research and teaching appeal to different people and require different skill sets. The REF and TEF are separate frameworks with distinct criteria, and a gulf exists between research-focussed and teaching-focussed academics, with two distinct communities developing (Tierney, 2016).
Key texts: [Aktar & Oxley 2019](../source-notes/aktar-oxley-2019-research-teaching-nexus.md)

### Position B: Research and Teaching are Interconnected
Others argue that teaching and research are interconnected through the "teaching-research" nexus. "Researcher-teachers" who combine their own research with high-quality teaching benefit from this dual role, and students benefit from engaging with this style of teaching. Linking research to teaching can drive improvement in both areas.
Key texts: [Aktar & Oxley 2019](../source-notes/aktar-oxley-2019-research-teaching-nexus.md)

## Key Texts
- [Aktar & Oxley 2019](../source-notes/aktar-oxley-2019-research-teaching-nexus.md) — supports Position B, acknowledges Position A

## Current State
The debate is ongoing. The authors of Aktar & Oxley 2019 advocate for strengthening the research-teaching link while acknowledging the institutional barriers (separate REF/TEF frameworks) that create a false dichotomy.

## Relevance to Paulo's Research
This debate matters for Paulo's interest in how research and practice inform teaching, particularly in music composition and pedagogy, where the relationship between research and teaching is often contested.
