# Widening Participation vs Social Mobility

## Framing

This debate concerns whether widening participation (expanding access to higher education for disadvantaged groups) actually produces social mobility, or whether the two are distinct and often conflated. At stake is whether education can compensate for society, and whether the rhetoric of human capital and employability obscures structural inequality.

## Positions

### Position A: Widening Participation Produces Social Mobility

Policymakers have typically linked widening participation in HE to improving opportunities for social mobility. The think-tank Policy Exchange asserts that colleges "can play a major role in widening participation and improving social mobility."
Key texts: [Avis & Orr 2016](../source-notes/avis-orr-2016-he-in-fe.md)

### Position B: Widening Participation Does Not Produce Social Mobility

While HE in FE has widened participation, it has not systematically enabled relative upward social mobility at least as measured by income. The differential in participation between the most and least advantaged remains intact, and HE in FE graduates earn less and are less likely to be in professional occupations than university graduates. "Education cannot compensate for society" (Bernstein).
Key texts: [Avis & Orr 2016](../source-notes/avis-orr-2016-he-in-fe.md)

## Key Texts

- [Avis & Orr 2016](../source-notes/avis-orr-2016-he-in-fe.md) — supports Position B, presents Position A as the policy rhetoric
- [Cameron et al 2018](../source-notes/cameron-2018-learning-gain-widening-participation.md) — provides empirical evidence of non-differentiated graduate outcomes for WP students, complicating the claim that WP cannot produce mobility
- [Ladson-Billings & Tate 1995](../source-notes/ladson-billings-tate-1995-critical-race-theory-education.md) — argues that race and property, not just class, structure educational inequity
- [Steele & Aronson 1995](../source-notes/steele-aronson-1995-stereotype-threat.md) — shows how stereotype threat undermines the test performance of stigmatized groups

## Current State

The debate is ongoing. The authors argue that conflating WP and social mobility elides the difference, and that education can be a resource in the struggle for social justice even if it cannot systematically produce social mobility.

## Relevance to Paulo's Research

This debate matters for Paulo's interest in the relationship between education, skill formation, and social structures, and whether education can transform or merely reflect unequal societies.
