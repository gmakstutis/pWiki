# Deep vs Surface Learning

## Framing

This debate concerns whether the distinction between deep learning (learning with understanding) and surface learning (rote learning) is a simple dichotomy or a more complex, contingent phenomenon. At stake is how teaching and assessment should be designed to promote deep learning, and whether a deep approach is universally desirable.

## Positions

### Position A: Deep Learning is Universally Desirable

Broad evaluations of higher education have called for a greater degree of deep learning relative to surface learning. The focus in the higher education literature is on ways of promoting and assessing deep learning. Constructive alignment aims to trap the learner into learning appropriately.
Key texts: [Beattie et al 1997](../source-notes/beattie-1997-deep-surface-learning.md) · [Biggs Constructive Alignment](../source-notes/biggs-constructive-alignment.md)

### Position B: The Deep-Surface Distinction is Complex and Contingent

The use of the deep-surface dichotomy generally oversimplifies. The distinction is relevant to several aspects of learning (intentions, styles, approaches, outcomes), and it is unrealistic to assume a deep approach is universally desirable, since it may be necessary to adopt a surface approach given the nature of the knowledge to be acquired. The deep-surface approach comprises only one of several components influencing a student's overall learning orientation.
Key texts: [Beattie et al 1997](../source-notes/beattie-1997-deep-surface-learning.md)

## Key Texts

- [Beattie et al 1997](../source-notes/beattie-1997-deep-surface-learning.md) — supports Position B, presents Position A as the dominant call
- [Biggs Constructive Alignment](../source-notes/biggs-constructive-alignment.md) — supports Position A
- [Cope 2003](../source-notes/cope-2003-entrepreneurial-learning-critical-reflection.md) — complicates the dichotomy by distinguishing higher-level from lower-level learning

## Current State

The debate is ongoing. Beattie et al. argue that a fuller understanding of the complex, composite, and contingent nature of deep and surface learning is required for successful intervention strategies.
