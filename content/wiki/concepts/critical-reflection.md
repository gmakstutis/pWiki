# Critical Reflection

## Definition

Reflection that challenges personal norms, assumptions and 'taken-for-granteds'. Within the adult learning framework of Mezirow, Marsick and Watkins, and Boud, reflection can be critical if it challenges personal norms, assumptions and taken-for-granteds. This is distinguished from 'critical' in the Critical Theory sense, which concerns challenging power relations in the pursuit of emancipatory social and political ideals. The reflection described here is more precisely 'inward' critical self-reflection (Kemmis, 1985), which reassesses the way we have posed problems and our own orientation to perceiving, knowing, believing, feeling and acting. Critical reflection is the outcome of, and inextricably linked to, higher-level learning triggered by discontinuous events.
