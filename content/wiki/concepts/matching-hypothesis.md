# Matching Hypothesis

## Definition
The hypothesis that students' learning is enhanced when a mode of instruction is used that matches their learning preference. Also known as the 'meshing' hypothesis, it implies that students learn more effectively when they receive instruction in a way that conforms to their preferred learning style. To confirm the hypothesis, studies must show a crossover interaction effect where students show higher achievement when their learning style matches the instructional mode and lower achievement when there is a mismatch. Pashler et al. (2009) identified the criteria for adequate evidence: multiple groups or conditions, random assignment, the same achievement test for all subjects, and a crossover interaction effect. The matching hypothesis has been widely refuted by methodologically sound research.
