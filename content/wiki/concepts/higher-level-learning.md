# Higher-Level Learning

## Definition
Fundamental learning that generates new understandings and new cognitive 'theories for action', forcing individuals to question their established ways of doing things. It is contrasted with lower-level learning, which is focused, routine, short-term and surface. Higher-level learning encompasses numerous forms described in different bodies of literature, including 'double-loop' learning (Argyris & Schön), 'transformative' learning (Mezirow), 'generative' learning (Senge), and 'higher-order' learning (Fiol & Lyles). Discontinuous events — crises, shocks, or 'disorienting dilemmas' — are powerful triggers for higher-level learning, which is the outcome of 'inward' critical self-reflection.
